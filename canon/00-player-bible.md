# Player Bible — what a new Royce employee can know

Public canon v1 (2026-10-11). Safe for any prompt. Source: `docs/source/world-bible-02-04-royce.md`.

## The setting
October 1977. North-central North Dakota, 40–60 miles from Minot. Royce Industries
is a privately owned research and development company whose headquarters, known
internally as **Station R**, occupy roughly 600 acres of isolated prairie. The campus
opened in 1972. By 1977 Royce is among the region's largest private employers. Its
revenue and ownership structure are hard to determine.

The surrounding country is real Cold War landscape: Minot Air Force Base is a
Strategic Air Command installation, Minuteman III missiles have been in the ground since
1970, and the dispersed missile complex stretches across the counties around Station R.
Local papers have carried stories about cattle mutilations since 1975, unmarked
helicopters, strange lights, and, more recently, a "Devil Cult". The Son of Sam arrest in
New York (August 1977) is in every newspaper. These are background facts, not plot.

## The company
Incorporated in Delaware in 1959 by industrialist **Edmund H. Royce**, who made his
fortune in military instrumentation and precision manufacturing. Early products:
avionics components, specialized sensors, industrial control systems, laboratory
instruments. In 1969 the company bought the North Dakota land and announced a major
research facility for high-energy physics, advanced materials, electromagnetic systems
and aerospace instrumentation.

Royce does not look sinister. Modern cafeteria, recreational association, company
baseball teams, respectable salaries, a remarkably generous pension, holiday newsletters,
sponsored science fairs. Relocation paid in full. An extraordinary confidentiality clause.

Things an attentive employee notices in the first weeks:
- Some research divisions produce no marketable products.
- Certain projects receive equipment far beyond their stated budgets.
- Restricted buildings have their own power supplies.
- There is a nighttime flight operation but no aviation department.
- One laboratory is supplied with biological material although the company says it
  discontinued animal research.
- Different departments issue different maps of the campus.

## The Department
Inside Royce there is an administrative unit formally designated the
**Department of Special Research and Development (DSRD)**. Employees call it The
Department. It coordinates sensitive projects, handles unusual technical requirements,
resolves interdepartmental disputes, and manages work requiring special clearance.
New hires are not told what it does. They are drawn into it.

## The eight departments
| Department | What the employee thinks they do | Primary gameplay |
|---|---|---|
| Advanced Materials | metallurgy and exotic alloys | lab experiments, prototypes, technical discoveries |
| Aerospace Systems | aviation and guidance | flight records, field tests, surveillance |
| Biological Sciences | veterinary and medical research | specimen analysis, ecological investigation, research ethics |
| Applied Physics | energy systems and instrumentation | experimental scheduling, energy resources, containment decisions |
| Information Systems | computing and statistical analysis | data correlation, intercepted signals, pattern discovery |
| Procurement & Logistics | purchasing and transportation | financial analysis, shipping, hidden transactions |
| Corporate Security | industrial security and investigations | investigations, access permissions, surveillance |
| Personnel & Administration | employment and internal policy | hiring, internal politics, governance, records, influence |

Each department has its own authority, resources, restricted information and
capabilities. No one sees the whole picture. Every player has something another player
needs.

## What is in the air (October 1977)
Cattle have been found dead and cut in ways ranchers cannot explain. Unmarked
helicopters are seen at night. The press talks about a Devil Cult; some locals talk
about the Vigil, or the Congregation of the Last Light, or just the Keepers. Older
people remember lights over the fields and a surveyor's story from 1883 that "the
ground is not empty here". A scientist has vanished. A technician is dead. And
someone slid a photograph under a new employee's door with a note on the back:
*They're not worshipping it. They're trying to keep it asleep.*

## Rhythm
One round per day: dispatch 9:00 AM Eastern, order lock 8:00 PM Eastern. Fourteen
rounds per episode. Twelve episodes in Season One, running from October 1977 into
spring 1978. PROPOSED for launch: a pilot of Episodes 1–3 with twelve players.
