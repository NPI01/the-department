## Document A — The 1921 Bell Expedition Journal (excerpt)

*Field book, octavo, pencil. Hand of Dr. Lucian Bell. Pages 41–44.*

**Thursday 13 October 1921. Mountrail County, near the Tagus siding.**

Arrived by the morning train with Mrs. Wren, Mr. Aldous and the Reverend Pell. The
property is the old Halvorsen place, a mile and a quarter south-west of the church.
The report that brought us here came from Halvorsen's widow: a sound beneath the
house "like a church full of people with the doors shut." We have heard this
description before, from Vetch's assistant in 1883, and I confess it is why I came.

The cellar is ordinary. Fieldstone walls, a coal chute on the east side, packed earth
floor. A concrete stair of twenty steps, newer than the house, poured I should think
by the county when the siding was built. At the foot, a concrete wall. I sounded it
with the hammer: solid. I lowered the plumb line through a gap at the floor's edge and
it paid out nine feet and stopped on stone. There is nothing here.

Mrs. Wren does not agree. She stood on the fourth step and would go no further. She
says the wall at the bottom "is not where it was yesterday," though we arrived today.
She says there is a second passage behind the coal chute, going left and down, and
that she has seen it. She drew it for me on the back of the train ticket. I have
pasted the ticket on the facing page. It is a corridor turning on itself, with a
chamber drawn as a circle of small marks. I asked her what the marks were. She said,
"Seats."

**Friday 14 October.**

Returned alone at first light to settle the matter. Counted the steps going down:
twenty-one. The transit tripod, set on the cellar floor for a bearing, took up a
vibration I could feel in the brass. Not the train; the train had passed. A low note,
steady, which continued when the wind outside stopped. I timed it against my watch
for six minutes and it did not vary. Coming up I counted the steps again: nineteen.
I attribute the discrepancy to fatigue and the poor light.

Mrs. Wren had left a note under my door at the boarding house. I copy it here:

> *Do not make them agree. Let Aldous say a shaft and the Reverend say a room and
> you say a wall. It is the agreeing that lets it finish. Keep the last light on.*
> *— C. W.*

I do not know what she means by it. I have filed the note with the property survey.

## Document B — Prairie Geological Services, Report PGS-69-044

```
PRAIRIE GEOLOGICAL SERVICES
A SUBSIDIARY OF ROYCE INDUSTRIES, INC.        REPORT NO. PGS-69-044
                                              DATE: 12 NOVEMBER 1969
SUBJECT: SUBSURFACE SURVEY, HALVORSEN PARCEL, TAGUS QUADRANGLE
PREPARED BY: W. Orr (Crew A), D. Lindqvist (Crew B)
CLEARANCE: PROJECT TIER 3                      COPY 2 OF 4
```

**1. Scope.** Two crews surveyed the disused cellar and stair on the Halvorsen parcel
on 4 and 5 November 1969, independently, under instruction not to confer. Crew A used
tape and transit. Crew B used tape, transit and the company's density gauge.

**2. Findings, Crew A (Orr).** Concrete stair, 20 risers, 7-inch rise, total descent
11 ft 8 in. Terminates at a poured concrete wall, 8 in. thick by sounding. Floor
packed earth over stone at 9 ft. No side passage. Sketch A attached.

**3. Findings, Crew B (Lindqvist).** Concrete stair, 47 risers, 7-inch rise, total
descent 27 ft 5 in. Terminates at an iron door, riveted, no handle on the near side,
set in a concrete frame. Corridor turns left before the door; not entered. Density
gauge indicates a void of not less than 1,200 cubic feet beyond the door at bearing
248°. Sketch B attached.

**4. Reconciliation.** The crews' tapes were checked against each other on the
surface and agree to a quarter inch over 100 ft. Both crews were sober. Crew A's
sketch and Crew B's sketch cannot both be correct. A third descent by Orr and
Lindqvist together, 6 November, found 20 risers and the wall. Lindqvist maintains
his count.

**5. Stratigraphy.** Core at the cellar floor passes directly from packed fill into
the Pierre shale at 9 ft. There is no soil horizon between. The two feet of topsoil
present everywhere else on the parcel is absent beneath the house, as if the house
had been set down on bare rock before the soil formed around it.

**6. Recommendation.** Continuous instrumentation of the cellar; monthly re-survey by
alternating crews; no excavation pending review. Crew B's Sketch B bears a resemblance
to Figure 3 of Engineering Reference 68-24 (restricted); Engineering should be asked
whether the reference was available to Crew B. It was not issued to this office.

```

                   cc: Office of the Chief Scientist (Dr. A. Vale)
                   cc: Corporate Security (H. Sloane)
                   14 NOV 1969  Filed, Tier 3.   Sketch B: REMOVED FROM FILE 2 DEC 1969 — H.S.
```

## Document C — Corporate Security, Threat Assessment: Occult Activity, Mountrail County

```
ROYCE INDUSTRIES — CORPORATE SECURITY DIVISION
TO:   File; Office of the Chairman
FROM: H. Sloane, Director
DATE: 28 October 1977
SUBJECT: Threat assessment — organized occult activity, Mountrail County
CLASSIFICATION: EXECUTIVE TIER 4 — DO NOT CIRCULATE
```

**1. Summary.** An organized group, self-described in intercepted material as "the
Vigil," is active within twelve miles of Station R and has recruited at least four
company employees. The group's activities include trespass on company land, animal
mutilation, nocturnal gatherings at the Halvorsen parcel near Tagus, and the
distribution of ritual diagrams. This office assesses the group as a security threat
to the April program and recommends the measures in section 6.

**2. Animal mutilations.** Since 1975 the county has recorded eleven cattle deaths with
"surgical" injuries. Sheriff Hollis attributes them to scavengers. Three of the eleven
occurred within a mile of the Halvorsen parcel. At two of the three, markings were
found on fence posts (Exhibit 1, photographs). The markings correspond to the diagram
at Exhibit 2, recovered from a pamphlet seized at the Tagus post office.

**3. Exhibit 2.** The diagram is a circle of twelve marks with a radial line. It
appears in the seized pamphlet, on the fence posts, and in a letter postmarked
Yonkers, N.Y., addressed to an employee of this company (Exhibit 3), in which the
writer connects it to "the symbol in the New York papers this summer." This office
assesses the diagram as the group's identifying mark.

**4. Internal penetration.** Badge records place four employees off campus on the
nights of the three gatherings observed by this office (6 Sept, 20 Sept, 4 Oct).
Two are in Biological Sciences, one in Information Systems, one in Personnel.
Names are held in the Director's file.

**5. Related matters.** The September transfer of Dr. E. Marsh was routine and is
unconnected. The July death of P. Greeley was an industrial accident and the file is
closed. This office notes that the group has circulated both events as evidence of
company wrongdoing.

**6. Recommendations.** Perimeter patrols doubled through April. Surveillance of the
Halvorsen parcel. Suspension of the four employees' clearances pending interview.
Liaison with the county to ensure the group is correctly characterized to the press.

H. Sloane

*Exhibit 2 (attached): a mimeographed diagram, a circle of twelve marks with one
radial line, hand-labelled "recovered pamphlet, Tagus P.O., 9/22/77."*
