# Season One — the story, episode by episode

SECRET CANON v1 (2026-10-18). Engine and GM only. PROPOSED until Nathan confirms. Written by
Claude at Nathan's request as the narrative companion to `16-season-one-chronology.md`: one
full paragraph per episode saying what happens, in the order it happens, on the path the
engine runs if the players do not bend it, with the places they can bend it named as they
come. Every fact is from the truth ledger, the arcs (`10`), the chronology (`16`) and the
built episodes (`07`, `13`, `15`, `17`); the OPEN items (what the Presence is, whether its
emergence is inevitable, the seventh threshold) stay open here. Dates follow the calendar in
`16`: three-day intermissions after every second episode.

## Episode 01 — Orientation (10 to 23 October 1977)

A cohort of new hires arrives at Station R on a Monday in October with their relocation paid,
their confidentiality clauses signed, and a campus map that does not match the one the next
department was issued. Vale's orientation memo tells them nothing about the Department they
have been hired into, and the first week's mail tells them everything the company would
rather they did not notice: Ely's requisition for a HARVEST lot that was never logged, written
as if Dr. Marsh were still expected back; Sloane's memo to Fisk about a Prairie Meridian
helicopter that landed three miles outside its corridor and missed the beacon; and the
sheriff's letter about Royce trucks beside a drained steer on Gary Vane's north pasture. Two
letters come from outside the fence, one from an old man in Tagus about a hum he hears in a
bell tower whose bell was removed in 1961, and one from Yonkers, New York, about a symbol in
the margins of an 1883 survey map. In Round 3 the cohort elects two of its own to the employee
seats on the Research and Safety Committees, the first power a new hire can hold. Hensley then
sponsors a motion that would make the Department report to the Board through Finance, and
the Board splits three to three with Walter Crane undecided; for five rounds Vale offers
clearance to anyone who lobbies against it, Crane waits to be shown money leaving without a
return, and Baines can be turned by proof that the night flights are being used for sampling
runs he never signed for. Meanwhile the three things the whole season will turn on are loose
in the building: Hensley has a copy of Marsh's September report, Greeley's notebook is sealed
in a locker in Building 2 with Fisk holding the key, and Procurement's 1974 invoices show
eleven loads of concrete delivered to a parcel near Tagus where nothing has ever been built.
The pressure clock climbs whatever the players do: unlogged vehicle entries, night flights
moved to two in the morning, stress leave, magnetic drift on the perimeter sensors, failed
calibrations citing conflicting coordinates, the sheriff's request for a statement, and in
the last two rounds rotated access codes and grounded flights. The Executive Assistant who
has been told in a sealed memo how the Board vote must go will notice, if they look, that the
instruction is signed by the Chairman's office and typed on the CFO's machine, which is the
episode's quiet twist: the Board is not what the seating chart says, and a new hire is being
used to prove it. On Round 9 the ORPHEUS field calibration runs, or does not, depending on
whether the Cult Recruit has managed to stop it. At the lock of Round 14 twelve private
objectives are judged, and the episode carries forward who holds Marsh's report, who saw the
map with Building 9 on it, how the motion went, who sits on the committees, and every promise
an NPC made in writing.

## Episode 02 — The Harvest (24 October to 6 November 1977)

The Minot Daily News runs the steer story on the Monday morning with a company helicopter in
the headline and Leonard Fisk quoted as a spokesman, which he is not and did not. Sheriff
Hollis writes to Sloane the same day: he is done with heads-up, he wants the name of whoever
signed out truck MP-14 on the night of the ninth, and he will ask the county attorney to ask
for him if he has to. Gary Vane writes to the newest hire, telling them to come and look at a
jar of tissue that has not turned in two weeks and then say lightning to his face. The
episode is a ledger. The county has eleven dead-cattle cases on its books since 1975 and they
are not one thing: four were HARVEST sampling under Ely's and Baines's signatures, two were
HARVEST sampling under nobody's, two were lightning and scavengers, and three on the Halvorsen
parcel in June 1976 are something the company cannot explain, including a three-pound metal
fragment, warm to the touch, that Hollis logged as unexploded ordnance, wrote up as referred
to Minot Air Force Base, and has kept in his own evidence locker for fifteen months. A player
who gets the sheriff's ledger, the PGS field log and the motor pool sign-out into one Case
File can sort every case, and will find that the truck that went out on the ninth came back
with sixty-one more miles on it than the Tagus round trip and a fuel chit initialled by
Gerald Voss. In the middle of it Greeley's death stops looking like an accident. His sister
writes from Fargo on Round 5 asking for the notebook the family was promised and never got;
a custodian clearing room 2-C finds a folded page on Round 7 and hands it to Security because
he was told to; Personnel's inventory of effects shows the notebook struck off and initialled
H.S.; and the Applied Physics daybook for 12 July is missing page 212, the one where Greeley
wrote that bench four answered him and that eleven minutes passed by the bench clock while
four passed on the wall. The truth the engine holds is that Greeley was not murdered. He ran
the session himself, alone, at an Ascendant's urging, and died of cardiac arrest, and three
people fled the room, and the custodian who found the page was one of them, which is why the
page he found is the one that clears Security. Ely writes to the Research Committee asking
for HARVEST's continuation and would rather not answer questions in writing, because Lot 77-B
went out under no signature at all. The pressure climbs from the paper to the sheriff's
written demand to Vane stopping his hay deliveries to the state veterinarian to a Bismarck
television crew at the Vane gate. The Research Committee's ballot on cooperating with the
county opens on day 9 and closes on day 13, with Vale hard against, Ely movable by proof that
77-B was unsigned, and Kovic for sale to anyone who tells him there is a fragment in a county
locker. The episode carries forward the sorted ledger, whether HARVEST was exposed or
shielded, who holds the notebook, and where the fragment is.

## Episode 03 — The Devil Cult (10 to 23 November 1977)

Sloane opens the episode with a memorandum to all staff announcing a review of outside
contacts, and Fisk follows it with a sketch of a symbol, a ring with a bar across its lower
third, that he says has been found four times on company property. Interviews by summons
begin in the first week. The company is purging a cult, and the player who examines Fisk's
memo closely will see that his sketch is traced, line for line, from the margin of the 1883
survey map in a county history, smudge and all. From day 5 the real invitations arrive. The
Vigil's comes in Elmer Bernard's hand, a folded half-sheet asking the reader to come to the
church at dusk and bring no light. The Congregation of the Last Light's comes as a card typed
on an IBM Selectric, the face used in Information Systems and nowhere else on campus, naming
the old grain elevator after midnight. Security's own comes on heavy bond with the RI
watermark, signed "the Vigil", naming the Building 2 locker room on Thursday, and anyone who
goes finds nobody there and a light on in the Security office across the lot; Security now
has a name. Each society writes once to whoever has earned it by what they have done:
visited the church, heard the tower hum, held the Vetch page, written to Bernard or Sayer,
noticed the unattended batch jobs, answered question 11 on a Form 14-B with anything but
NONE. Accepting joins; membership is never automatically betrayal of Royce. At the lock of
day 7 Sloane names for interview everyone Security has noticed three times, everyone who was
reported, and everyone who took the bait, and the named lose Records and restricted access
for two rounds. The list in his office was initialled and then two names were added in
another ink. The Safety Committee's ballot on authorizing internal surveillance opens on day
6 and closes on day 11; Sloane is for it, Baines leans with him until he is shown that the
exhibits are the company's own work, Hensley leans against and votes it down if she is given
the forgery in writing. The forgery is the episode's turn: the Security report on occult
activity dates its seized pamphlet to the Tagus post office in September 1977, and the Tagus
post office closed with the last business in 1976; Exhibit 3, the ritual diagram, is drawn on
Royce drafting film with a 1977 batch number in the corner. The Devil Cult the company is
purging is the company's own invention, run by Sloane since 1976 to paint the Vigil as
Satanists. The second, quieter turn is in the two late names on the purge list: Armitage
added them, to move two of Thorne's young recruits out of Records before the next episode,
and only a player who compares the list to its initials will know that a Keeper is using
Security's purge as a tool. The episode ends with two desks cleared and carries forward who
belongs to what, who reported whom, whether the forgery is known, and who was purged.

## Episode 04 — The Church at Tagus (24 November to 7 December 1977)

The episode opens on Thanksgiving with the campus at half strength. On the afternoon before,
a Prairie Geological Services surveyor named Lyle Brandvold hung a plumb line, a steel tape
and a weighted line from one bolt on the lower landing of the utility cellar behind St. Olaf
and read eleven feet, forty feet, and no bottom at three hundred, all at 1640. He teletyped
the readings, wrote that he was going down to check the tape, and did not report. PGS told
Voss and nobody else, because to Security PGS does not exist; the sheriff learns it from the
surveyor's wife, who called the PGS number and got a woman in Royce's Procurement office.
Bernard writes that a man came up out of the ground on Thursday night, shut the company's
door and stood with his hand on it, would not come into the church, and walked west on foot.
In the first three rounds the paper trail opens: Brandvold's field book is in the truck at
the PGS yard, with a second hand on its last line; the PGS payroll pays a surveyor named
Sandvik against a badge number the badge office never issued; the land records at Stanley
show the church parcel conveyed to PGS in 1969 for a dollar, signed for the buyer by Voss and
witnessed by F. Baines, Col., USAF. Armitage sends Personnel to call on Mrs. Rud at the farm
west of the siding as a courtesy. Vale asks for one honest witness to stand on the landing
for ten minutes and bring back the church clock's time against their watch; Kovic offers a
hand magnetometer and a place on JANUS for the numbers. Sloane sponsors the Safety
Committee's motion to pour the cellar shut and fence the parcel; Baines objects on
contractual grounds, refuses to name the contract, and tells the committee to apply to the
CFO for the index and then explain themselves to the Chairman. Whoever goes down reaches the
wall at about twenty steps and finds two tapes clipped to one bolt, one paid out to eleven
feet and one to forty, both taut; Applied Physics will say that all three readings were
correct measurements of a space that does not have one depth. The ballot closes on day 7.
Under the Halvorsen instrument hut, two men go down a hatch at 2330 most nights and the pen
recorder stops for eleven minutes at 0016, and the log down there shows one visitor by name
in eight years: E. Marsh, D.A. sign-out, 14 August. On day 9 Mrs. Rud writes to whoever has
been asking after the surveyor politely: he is not lost, he came up the same night, come
alone and in daylight and without a form. On day 12 a federal contracting officer named Ashby
gives the Chairman notice of inspection of Verification Site T, and on day 13 Brandvold
sends a page from his field book dated three days in one ink: I was down there three days, it
was one night, don't let them pour with anyone inside. The twist is in Hensley's cabinet: the
1969 contract index lists the three depths the surveyor took last week. Royce did not find
the hole. The land was bought because of it, with federal money, and the company was built
around a door. The episode carries forward sealed or open, who holds the index, who stood on
the stairs, and whether anyone met the man at the farm.

## Episode 05 — The Unfinished Descent (11 to 24 December 1977)

Someone goes past the wall. If the committee sealed the door, the way down is the ventilation
shaft the 1969 invoices paid for, which comes up under a steel plate in the Halvorsen
windbreak, and the Congregation knows it and has already written to its members to say so.
If the door is open, the way down is the door, by Vale's authorization or by night. Either
way, on day 4 a player stands where Brandvold stood and finds that the wall is not a wall
from below, and the first rule of the stairway becomes something they know rather than
something they have read: how far you get depends on what you believe is down there. A
descender who expects something specific goes further and sees it; one who expects nothing,
or two things at once, stops near the twentieth step. Two instruments carried on the same
descent disagree by an amount that grows with every week of the season. Bradley, flying
Sector 4 on day 6, sees an orange light over the church cellar and files nothing. On day 7
the two societies make their offers to their members. Thorne's cell offers the Seven
Descents, a doctrine of thresholds of which the first, second and fourth are real and
measurable, the third and sixth are invention, and the fifth, that a collective consciousness
is required, is deliberately false. Mrs. Rud offers the Keepers' practice: write your own
account of the night before you read anyone else's, and make sure the accounts disagree.
This is the episode's real vote and it is never put to a committee. Three incompatible
accounts of a night recorded in the same round cap the stairs at the first threshold for
everyone; consistent accounts feed the thing below. On day 10 a descender who has reached the
fourth threshold meets someone on the stairs who is not supposed to be anywhere: Evelyn
Marsh, alive, who was last seen on 29 September and whose file says she transferred out of
state. Nobody can prove it, and she says very little, and the encounter is the first time a
player sees that the stairway draws on the absent. The episode's twist belongs to whoever
descends alone: a single person gets further than a group, which is the opposite of what the
Congregation preaches and of what CHOIR has been built on since 1973, and Thorne will deny it
to anyone who tells him. The Research Committee's ballot on authorizing experiments on the
passage opens on day 7 and closes on Christmas Eve, with Vale for, Sloane's allies against,
and Kovic wanting his instruments down there regardless. On the last night the engine
changes one date on a document in a descender's own Case File and says nothing about it; the
first disturbance of the season, and it is the player's. The episode carries forward the
rules the players have learned as knowledge, the Index's movement, a disturbance row on
everyone who went past the second threshold, and whether Marsh was seen.

## Episode 06 — The Sons of Sam (25 December 1977 to 7 January 1978)

The Christmas mail brings cards with the ring-and-bar symbol on them, and letters from Martin
Sayer in Yonkers that copy the imagery the newspapers have printed all autumn from the Son of
Sam case: the dog, the .44, the letters to Breslin. Fisk builds his theory from them in a
week. By day 5 the symbol has turned up in three places that have nothing to do with each
other and everything to do with the stairway: in the Keepers' account book at the Rud farm,
dated 1921 and in Clara Wren's hand; in a CHOIR signal-recognition diagram from Information
Systems; and in a Vigil manuscript seized in the purge. Fisk reads this as a transcontinental
Satanic network reaching from New York to the missile fields, and the Chairman, who wants the
cult story out of his company by any door, sponsors a Board motion to hand Security's whole
cult file to the federal liaison, Daniel Mercer, who already believes, wrongly, that the
occult society runs Royce. The motion closes on day 11. On day 9 a letter arrives with a
Yonkers postmark that has come through the internal mail, which means Sayer's correspondent
is on campus. Players who trace the correspondence find two things. The first is that Sayer
has no connection to the murders at all; he is a paranormal magazine writer who dresses his
letters in whatever the papers are printing. The second is that his Royce correspondent is
Julian Thorne, Director of Information Systems, who typed the Congregation's invitation card
on his own Selectric, who runs the unattended batch jobs on the nights men are on the stairs,
and who signs the players' access authorizations. The cell leader is a department head, and
this is the season's midpoint. A quieter discovery sits beside it: the extra name on the
October roster, the ghost employee Thorne falsified a 1976 audit to hide, is Ole Sandvik, the
PGS man who will not talk, paid since 1969 under a badge number that was never issued because
he is the Keeper who drew Sketch B of the stairs for the 1969 survey and went down ahead of
Brandvold to keep him from going far. Sloane wants the file kept inside; Mercer wants it;
Haugen at the Minot Daily News wants a quote, and on day 13 Fisk's theory either reaches the
paper or is stopped at the gate. If nobody acts the file goes to Mercer and confirms his
mistake, and Fisk's theory becomes the company's. The episode carries forward whether Thorne
is exposed and to whom, whether the file left the fence, and what Fisk is now worth.

## Episode 07 — SENTINEL (11 to 24 January 1978)

Mercer's file lands on day 2, with whoever he has come to trust or, failing that, with Sloane.
It names PROGRAM SENTINEL, created in 1969 to verify, assess and contain, and it is the first
time the players see written down what the contract index only implied: the federal money
that built Station R, bought Tagus and pays for OMEGA. On day 6 the contracting officer,
Ashby, writes to demand acceleration, and his letter carries a date, April 1978, which no
player has seen on paper before. Everything since October has been preparation for a test
eleven weeks away. Hensley's numbers will not close. On day 9 she puts it in a memorandum:
OMEGA has outrun its contractual authority by a figure the ledger page for appropriation 77-S
has been hiding since September, and either the sponsor knows and does not care or the
sponsor does not know and will. Crane sponsors the motion the whole episode has been
building toward, a vote of no confidence in the Chairman or in the Chief Scientist, which
closes on day 13. The Board starts four to three for continuing OMEGA as it is, so one
persuaded or dismissed director flips it, and the deciding director is Crane himself, who can
be bought with a seat on the Research Committee for his nominee and who reads anything about
money. Players choose a side, in writing: the sponsor, who wants the work faster; Royce, who
wants a product and a contract and refuses Vale's reading of what the apparatus is; Vale,
who believes he is midwifing the next stage of humanity; or the officials outside the fence
who want the program dismantled. On day 10 the second turn lands. Baines, the stiffest
loyalist on the Board, the retired colonel who warns juniors against unauthorized curiosity,
is SENTINEL's officer inside the fence, and his contingency for the Basin site, a plan to
seize or destroy the apparatus if the company loses control of it, is written down and in his
office. No weapon exists and none will; the contingency is paper, and paper is enough. If
nobody acts, leadership holds and the sponsor's acceleration is accepted. On day 14 the
loser's office is cleared, whoever that is. The episode carries forward who leads Royce, how
far the federal bid for control has gone, and who now knows the date.

## Episode 08 — The Minot Winter (25 January to 7 February 1978)

The researchers of the International Geophysical Coordination Commission arrive in the
coldest week of the year, led by a Danish field scientist named Lund, carrying questions that
were written by someone inside Royce's own history. On day 4 a leaflet appears in Minot
warning that foreign troops will occupy the county under United Nations authority, and the
player who compares it to the files will find it is word for word a draft written in
Sloane's office in 1976, a rumour the company invented to discredit the cult and that has now
come home to arm a militia. On day 7, the act's climax, three helicopters are over Tagus in
one night: Bradley's Prairie Meridian aircraft on a calibration run he did not want to fly, a
federal verification team's, and a third that Doyle's radar at the base never saw, that made
no rotor noise, and that the engine does not call an aircraft at all. The helicopter ledger
is sorted the way the cattle ledger was, case by case, and the third case stays open. On day
10 the militia is at the fence, and Gary Vane is among them. The Research Committee's ballot
on admitting or expelling the IGCC observer closes on day 11. On day 13 a Congregation
pamphlet floats the speculation that a nuclear-scale energy release near a conditioned site
could force convergence, and Ashby pays Kovic's JANUS laboratory a visit and asks a question
Kovic will not answer; nobody has a weapon and nobody will acquire one, but the idea is now
in the county, and one faction believes it would destroy the phenomenon rather than complete
it. The episode's twist is Sarah Ely. The young idealist who runs HARVEST, who wants Royce's
work to have medical applications, who asked new hires to help with routine tissue sampling
in their first week, is the IGCC's source inside the fence, feeding the Commission sanitized
data because she believes the convergence is natural evolution and that the world should
know. Her letters are in Lund's case. If nobody acts the observer is expelled and the militia
pressure rises; players can admit Lund, quiet or stoke the militia, work out which helicopter
was which, and contain the nuclear rumour or let it spread. The episode carries forward the
militia as a standing pressure, Lund inside or outside the fence, the rumour's status, and
whether Ely has been named.

## Episode 09 — The Omega Hypothesis (11 to 24 February 1978)

Marsh's September report resurfaces on day 2 from wherever it has been since October, and
the engine knows exactly whose drawer that was. Its claims are what they were: OMEGA has
compromised the integrity of its own data, the company's earliest records were falsified,
the chamber should be suspended pending independent review. Letters begin arriving from
Miriam Kessler, who resigned from OMEGA in 1973 warning of a feedback loop and has been
missing since; she is the IGCC's unnamed source and the reason the Commission is in North
Dakota, and she wants Ely to stop. On day 7 the newspapers report that John Carr has died at
Minot Air Force Base. It is a real death and nobody in the fiction caused it, but Fisk's
nationwide theory had Carr in it, and the news either doubles his certainty or collapses it,
and it unsettles every other theory in the building for the same reason. On day 10 Evelyn
Marsh walks through the front gate of Station R with Dorothy Armitage beside her. She has
been at a Keeper property since the week Sloane opened his investigation; Armitage got her
out and Rud drove her there; she has descended the stairway once. The Vetch page that
arrived at her old office in October with the instruction to find the original date was sent
by Marsh herself, and now she explains it: the page's date had been revised, the way records
near the apparatus are revised, and the copy that carries the original date is in the chamber
at the bottom of the stairs, dated 1984. She has been there. She knows what the 1984 minutes
say the 1978 vote was. The Research Committee's ballot on resuming full OMEGA preparations
or suspending them closes on day 13, and the question is whether Marsh or her report gets
before the committee first and whether Vale, who disputes every line of it, can keep her out.
Sloane wants her in a room. On day 14 CHOIR's prediction test names the next morning's
headline, correctly. The second turn is one no NPC delivers. Every Form 14-B the players have
filed with a non-NONE answer to question 11 has been copied to Sublevel Nine under "Refer
D-SRD", and those are consistent records of anomalous experience, which is what the thing
below needs; the Index has been rising all season on the players' own paperwork. If nobody
acts the preparations resume on schedule. The episode carries forward Marsh's status, whether
Kessler has been named, the vote, and where the Index is heading.

## Episode 10 — Drawing 68-24 (25 February to 10 March 1978)

A player who holds both Drawing 68-24, the blueprint for the Convergence Chamber that came
out of Operation LANTERN on the night of the 1968 Minot sightings, and a survey of the Tagus
stairway finds that they are the same geometry. On day 3 Armitage, shown the drawing,
recognizes it as Clara Wren's 1921 diagram and tells one player so. On day 6 a core from the
older concrete in Sublevel Nine comes back from Kovic's or Elias Thorne's laboratory dated
1921, half a century before the building above it was poured, and its marks match no
construction plan because the older section is not construction: it is the chamber from the
1984 records, propagated backward and only partly realized. Royce built a second entrance to
the stairway without knowing it. The chamber beneath the stairs can be reached at the seventh
threshold, which nobody has managed, or by a procedure the players can now work out: two
people at the fourth threshold at the same hour, one on the stairs at Tagus and one in
Sublevel Nine, recording the same account. It opens by agreement, which is why no faction
has done it alone, and on day 8 it is reached or reported. What is in it enters play on day
13: Royce records dated 1984, an experiment report, personnel who have not yet joined the
company, and the minutes of a 1978 Research Committee vote on activating OMEGA, in which one
employee seat is held by a player, by name. The minutes end: the prior record was incorrect;
this is the third occurrence. The loop has run twice before and both times the record was
wrong. Every document in the chamber is now a prophecy with a predicate and a reliability
class, and players can fulfil, prevent or falsify each one. The Board's motion on who holds
the drawing and the chamber keys closes on day 12, with Vale, who built it, wanting the keys,
Royce wanting them as property, and Armitage wanting the drawing out of Vale's hands
altogether. Thorne wants to be inside the chamber. Marsh and Rud want the records read and
then contradicted. If nobody acts the drawing and the keys stay with the scientific
leadership. The episode carries forward whose hands the prophecy documents are in, whether
the loop is understood, and who has been inside.

## Episode 11 — The Threshold (14 to 27 March 1978)

Every faction moves at once, toward a vote the 1984 minutes already describe. On day 4, the
seventeenth of March, one year to the day since the chamber ran for eleven minutes and a
voice told three witnesses they were not the first to build this, the apparatus runs for
eleven minutes again, unbidden; nobody scheduled it, the Index did. On day 6 Lund demands an
inspection for the Commission. On day 9 Ashby serves the seizure paper on whoever holds Tagus,
with Baines's men behind him, and the site changes hands or does not. The Keepers push for
containment and Rud asks three players for three incompatible accounts of the vote, written
before the vote, and keeps them; the Congregation pushes to open, and Thorne wants to be on
the occupancy list; Hensley wants suspension; Royce wants ownership; Vale wants activation;
Sloane has to decide whose orders Security now takes. The Research Committee's vote on
activating the apparatus closes on day 13, and it is the vote the chamber predicted. The
minutes name a chair who, in March 1978, does not sit on the committee, and who that is
depends on how the no-confidence vote went in January; the engine fills the name from the
world as it stands. Players who hold the prophecy documents can try to fulfil the recorded
outcome, prevent it, or make the record false, and the record itself says that the last two
attempts to do the third failed. Brandvold goes to the cellar alone on the night of the vote
to make sure nobody is inside, and is found there at dawn. On day 14 the occupancy list is
fixed: who will be inside the chamber on the night of the test, and what each of them
believes is below, because by the stairway's first rule that belief decides what they will
find. If nobody acts the vote goes the way the scientific leadership wants. The episode
carries forward the vote against the prophecy, who is inside, and who holds Tagus.

## Episode 12 — The Opening (28 March to 10 April 1978)

Nothing is scripted. On day 3 the company newsletter announces the April demonstration to
every tray on campus. Day 7 is the last round a motion can move the authorization. On day 10
whoever holds Tagus decides the stairway's fate: sealed, guarded, open, or collapsed. On day
13, Sunday the ninth of April, the full-scale test runs, or is sabotaged, or is seized, or is
dismantled, or something nobody planned happens instead, and the engine resolves which from
the state the season has left: the Index band, the apparatus intact or modified or sabotaged
or seized, who may legally run it, who is inside the chamber and what their expectation
profiles say, whether the Keepers' practice is active at Tagus, and whether the March vote
matched the 1984 record. One thing is fixed across every ending. At the test hour the chamber
beneath Station R and the stairway beneath Tagus are one place, as they have been since the
company built its second entrance without knowing it, and whoever is in the chamber and
whoever is on the stairs see each other. What they see is the ending. In the Quiet, the
apparatus is dismantled or the test cancelled with the Index low, nothing happens, the
sponsor pulls its money, Royce is sold or folds, and the records go on drifting. In Eleven
Minutes, the test runs at a middling Index with contradictory accounts, March 17 happens at
scale, and one person on the occupancy list does not come out. In the Third Occurrence, the
vote matched the record, the loop closes, and the 1984 documents now read as this cohort's
own account of the night; the prior record that was incorrect is theirs. In the Falsified
Record, the vote was made false with the practice active, the prophecy documents change
overnight, and the Index falls hard. In the Seizure, SENTINEL holds the site at the hour and
the test runs under federal control or is stopped by it. In the Opening, with a high Index,
consistent accounts and someone at the seventh threshold, the two entrances connect, the
upper flight collapses, and what is emerging is not answered. Every ending hands the same
three things to Season Two: CHOIR's output, which has produced coordinates that match the
Crestone correspondence in the San Luis Valley; a letter postmarked Crestone, Colorado,
to one player, from Marsh or Kessler or Thorne or Lund or an unknown hand, by ending; and a
facts row that says what the Presence now is. On the morning of day 14 each player's last
dispatch carries their own Form 14-B, question 11, answered in a hand they recognize and dated
1984.
