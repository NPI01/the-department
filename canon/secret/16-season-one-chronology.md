# Season One — chronology, accomplishments, climaxes and twists

SECRET CANON v1 (2026-10-18). Engine and GM only. PROPOSED throughout until Nathan
confirms. Written by Claude at Nathan's request on top of `04-season-one-arc.md` (the
spine), `10-episode-arcs.md` (per-episode arcs), the truth ledger, the allegiance matrix,
the cast sheets and the three built episodes (`13`, `15`, `world/episode-0*.ts`). Where
`10` arranges canon into a beginning, middle and end, this doc says what the players must
*do* in each episode for the season to move, where the mini-climaxes sit, which hidden
truth each episode turns over, and what Episode 12 resolves into for Season Two. Every
new fact is marked PROPOSED and keeps the ledger's DECIDED and OPEN items as they are.

## Where development stands (18 October 2026)

- **Episodes 1–3 are built to the engine**: world files, mail, governance, goals, the
  societies and the purge. Three simulated seasons have run on them.
- **Episode 4 exists as one paragraph** (`10 §Episode 04`) and Bible 04's round bands.
  No world file, no beat sheet, no goals, no public pack, no ballot leans, and the two
  people it cannot run without (the surveyor and the Keeper at the farm) have no names.
- **The cast is round for three episodes and then stops.** Nineteen of twenty-three NPCs
  have full sheets (voice, wants, never-says, a checkable lie, fears) and every sheet's
  `ladder` ends at `episode_3`. Nothing tells the engine what Vale, Armitage, Thorne or
  Baines want from a player in Episode 4 or Episode 9. Marsh, Sayer, Crane and Kessler
  have no sheet at all, and Marsh carries Act III.
- **The sims say the story is not pulling.** Across sim-ep1 to sim-ep3 the goals met went
  2 (partial), 2, 0. In the third run four goals failed at Round 14 and no synthetic
  player assembled a case across episodes. The goals are evidence predicates with no
  character wanting them met; the chronology below gives each episode's "must" an NPC
  who asks for it.

So: Episode 3 is developed enough to ship in the pilot. Episode 4 is not developed at
all, and the NPC layer has to be extended before it can be, because Episode 4 is the
first episode whose crisis happens off campus, among people the players have only read
letters from.

## The five threads

Each thread is a question the players can answer only by engaging with people, and each
turns over in a fixed episode. A thread's twist is told to one player first (whoever did
the thing that uncovers it) and to everyone else only through that player or the pressure
clock. That is the game: the player who finds a truth decides who learns it.

Act climaxes, as distinct from episode climaxes: **Episode 4, Round 14** (the ground is
real and the company was built around it), **Episode 8, Round 7** (three helicopters in
one night and one of them is not an aircraft), **Episode 12, Round 13** (the test).

## The twelve episodes

Each episode keeps Bible 02's shape: Rounds 1–2 open, 3–4 contradict, 5–6 escalate, **7 a
collective decision**, 8–10 uncover, 11–12 bargain, **13 an operation or disclosure that
puts someone in danger**, 14 resolve and carry forward. "Must" is what has to happen for
the season to move; if the players do not do it, "if nobody acts" in `10` does it to
them, worse. "Pull" names the NPC whose letter asks for it.

### Episode 04 — The Church at Tagus (Nov 24 – Dec 7)
The act's climax and the first episode off campus. Everything below is PROPOSED; the
canon it rests on is `02-tagus` (the three layers, rule 1 "depth is a function of
expectation", rule 3 "no one is lost for a round unless they choose to stay", the three
former employees), Bible 04 §11's round bands, and `10 §Episode 04`.

**Opening (R1–3).** Thanksgiving. Prairie Geological Services reports its field surveyor
**Lyle Brandvold** overdue to Voss, not to Security, because PGS does not exist to
Security. His last field message gives three depths for one void under the utility
cellar by St. Olaf: eleven feet, forty feet, and "no bottom at three hundred feet of
line". Bernard writes that a man came up out of the ground on Thursday night and walked
to the church and would not come in. Hollis takes a missing-person call from a wife in
Minot. Available: Brandvold's field book (in the PGS truck in the yard), Document B (the
1969 survey, two maps), the PGS payroll (Voss's 1974 lie), and in Hensley's cabinet a
contract index whose one Tagus line reads *Verification Site T*.
**Middle (R4–10).** Competing investigations. The hatch in the Halvorsen instrument hut
(seen in Episode 2) leads to the monitoring room: instruments, a surveillance log, and a
sign-out that puts Marsh at the site in August with Armitage's initials. Intermediaries
appear: **Agnes Rud**, 67, who keeps the Keepers' farm west of Tagus and the account
book "as it should be"; and a typed card from the Congregation, *come down with us*.
**R7: the Safety Committee votes S-77-004, closure of the Tagus utility structure.**
Leans: Sloane +1 (contain), Voss +0.6 (bury the money), Armitage +0.8 (a Keeper seals
what she can), Baines −1 (SENTINEL wants it open), Hensley −0.2 (wants the contracts read
first), Kovic by proxy −0.5 (wants instruments down there), plus the employee seat.
R8–10: whoever goes down, by permission or by night, reaches the wall at about twenty
steps and two instruments give two depths. Brandvold's three readings were all correct.
That is the first stairway rule, learned by standing on it.
**Close (R11–14).** If sealed, Security pours concrete on R12 and on R14 the new surface
carries marks that match no plan (the Sublevel Nine echo, a year early). If open, a
federal contracting officer, **Conrad Ashby**, arrives on R13 to inspect Verification
Site T, and the site has an owner nobody at Royce elected. R13 either way: Brandvold is
found at the Rud farm, alive, refusing to return, and writes to one player (whoever held
his field book or stood on the stairs): *I was down there three days. It was one night.
Don't let them pour with anyone inside.* The dates in his field book have changed.
**Must.** Someone enters the installation. The seal is decided (R7). Control of the site
is recorded at R14 as a facts row: Security, Research, PGS (Voss), the Keepers, or
SENTINEL. Optional but season-critical: the contract index reaches the Board or a player
keeps it (it is Episode 7's fuse).
**Twist (R10).** The SENTINEL contracts are dated 1969 and already contain the stairway's
depth readings. Royce did not find the hole; the land was bought because of it, with
federal money, and the company was built around it. Act I's question ("what is this
company?") is answered: a contractor standing on a door.
**Pull.** Voss (wants PGS to stay a line item; breaks under an inventory demand) · Sloane
(wants the surveyor found before the sheriff finds him) · Baines (wants it open and
quiet) · Armitage (wants it sealed and wants one player to meet Rud) · Vale (wants it

Bernard (saw the man come up) · Rud (wants three accounts of one night from whoever
went down) · Brandvold (wants his field book back and his name off the PGS payroll) ·
Hollis (a missing person is his, not Royce's).
**New people.** Brandvold; Rud; the three former PGS employees, **Ole Sandvik** (refuses
to talk), **Curtis Mahlum** (says they were looking for a tunnel), **Dennis Pfeifer** (says

1. **Every "must" has a face.** A goal is a letter from someone who wants it, not a
   predicate in a drawer. The Pull lines are the letter-writers; their sheets should say
   what they offer and what they do if ignored.
2. **Cases run across episodes.** The contract index found in 04 is judged in 07; the
   accounts recorded in 05 decide the Index in 09; the name read in 10 is the seat voted
   in 11. Goal predicates need a "judged through Episode N" the way ep1-6 already has.
3. **Round 13 is seen by everyone.** The operation or disclosure that puts someone in
   danger goes in every dispatch, so a player who did nothing still knows the world moved.
4. **Twists are found, then told.** The engine gives each twist to the player whose order
   uncovers it and otherwise lets it reach the cohort through the pressure clock. The
   player chooses who learns it. If no player finds it by the round given above, the
   pressure band carries it to everyone, worse.
