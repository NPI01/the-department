## Episode 01 — Orientation
**Logline.** A cohort arrives at Station R and finds that the company's records do not
agree with each other, while the Board fights over who DSRD answers to.

**Opening.** The Round 1 pack: offer letter, Vale's orientation memo, the
confidentiality clause, department maps that disagree about Building 7. Three
irregularities (Ely's lost HARVEST lot, Sloane's NIGHTJAR memo, the sheriff's drained
steer) and letters from Bernard and Sayer. In Round 3 the cohort elects two of its own
to the committee employee seats.

**Middle.** Hensley's motion B-77-001 would make DSRD report to the Board through
Finance. Vale courts opponents with clearance recommendations; Crane can be moved by
financial exposure; Baines by proof that NIGHTJAR flights serve HARVEST sampling. In
Round 6 Sayer's letter with a Royce building layout reaches the Cult Recruit. The vote
closes in Round 7.

**Close.** The ORPHEUS field calibration in Round 9; failed prototypes and conflicting
map coordinates; the sheriff wants a statement; access codes rotate and the night
flights are grounded.

**If nobody acts.** The pressure clock climbs all seven tiers; Marsh's report,
Greeley's notebook and the Tagus invoices stay where they are.

**How players bend it.** Who wins the employee seats; whether B-77-001 passes;
mitigating each pressure tier; pairing up on the twelve private objectives.

**Carries forward.** Who holds Marsh's report; who saw the second map; the motion's
outcome; the seat holders; Index movement; promises made in writing.

## Episode 02 — The Harvest
**Logline.** A rancher's dead steer with a Royce helicopter overhead puts HARVEST's
authority on trial, and Greeley's death stops looking like an accident.

**Opening.** A cattle case lands on the company's doorstep with a Royce helicopter in
the story. Each case is its own ledger row: HARVEST sampling (authorized or not),
ordinary causes, or something Royce cannot explain. Aerial activity is reported near
Tagus.

**Middle.** HARVEST's sampling authority is challenged. Greeley's notebook surfaces: his
effects hold geometric drawings, correspondence with an unidentified religious group,
and a final laboratory entry describing a test the company insists never happened. The
sheriff has buried cases to protect ranchers and filed debris from one site as
ordnance; it is R-1 class material. A rancher keeps a tissue sample that will not rot.

**Close.** The Research Committee decides whether to cooperate with the sheriff and
disclose HARVEST.

**If nobody acts.** HARVEST stays shielded; Security's account of Greeley stands; the
debris stays in the sheriff's locker labelled ordnance.

**How players bend it.** Establishing which cases are HARVEST and which are not;
getting the notebook or the debris into the right hands; carrying the committee vote
either way. Truth the engine holds: Greeley died during an unauthorized CHOIR-style
session he ran himself, and Sloane concealed it; Lot 77-B was sampled without
authority.

**Carries forward.** The first cattle case ledger; HARVEST exposed or shielded.

## Episode 03 — The Devil Cult
**Logline.** Security purges the cult it invented, real invitations arrive in the
middle of it, and nobody can tell which approaches are genuine.

**Opening.** Sloane's purge of suspected sympathizers. Fisk produces a symbol and a
theory.

**Middle.** The first invitations to the Vigil arrive (Round 5 in the season arc). Some
are genuine Keeper or Ascendant approaches, some are Fisk's or Sloane's provocations,
some come from a rival group. A forged ritual diagram turns up in a security file: part
of the disinformation campaign Sloane has run since 1976.

**Close.** The Safety Committee authorizes or blocks Security's internal surveillance.

**If nobody acts.** Surveillance is authorized; the forgery stays filed as evidence;
the two occult groups stay one "Devil Cult" in the company's eyes.

**How players bend it.** Accept, refuse, report or infiltrate an invitation; expose the
forgery; vote the surveillance down or up. Membership is never automatically betrayal
of Royce.

**Carries forward.** Who is a member; who reported whom; whether the forgery is known.

## Episode 04 — The Church at Tagus
**Logline.** A Royce surveyor vanishes after reporting a void beneath an old utility
structure near the church, and the company's quiet land purchases come into the open.

**Opening (Rounds 1–3).** The missing surveyor: his last message has depth
measurements that contradict each other. Invoices, personnel records, local accounts
and technical reports become available. Procurement's payments to Prairie Geological
Services point to off-books construction at Tagus.

**Middle (Rounds 4–10).** Competing investigations: players find the Royce
installation and meet Vigil intermediaries. Corporate Security wants the incident
contained, research wants the site studied, the Vigil wants the work halted, an
Ascendant faction calls the opening a gateway. SENTINEL contracts are found in the
books. In Round 7 the Safety Committee votes on sealing the entrance.

**Close (Rounds 11–14).** The consequences of exploration, sabotage or containment grow
more severe; at Round 14 control of the site, any recovered knowledge and its
continuing risks become permanent.

**If nobody acts.** Security contains the incident; the installation stays hidden
behind its subsidiary.

**How players bend it.** Seal the entrance or keep it open; expose or protect Prairie
Geological Services and the procurement record; take the SENTINEL contracts to the
Board.

**Carries forward.** Sealed or open; who controls the site.

## Episode 05 — The Unfinished Descent
**Logline.** Someone goes down the stairway, with or without permission, and the
instruments disagree about what they found.

**Opening.** The first authorized or covert descent. Depth follows expectation:
descenders who believe something specific go further; contradictory belief stops them
near the twentieth step.

**Middle.** Instruments disagree by design. A Keeper intermediary offers the
containment practice of keeping incompatible accounts; the Ascendants offer the Seven
Descents, a doctrine part true, part invented, part planted.

**Close.** The Research Committee decides whether to authorize experiments on the
passage.

**If nobody acts.** No descent is authorized; covert descents still raise the Index and
leave disturbances on those who make them.

**How players bend it.** Descend or refuse; record contradictory accounts (which caps
descents) or consistent ones (which feed the phenomenon); authorize, restrict or ban
experiments.

**Carries forward.** The stairway rules players have learned; Index movement;
disturbances on descenders.

## Episode 06 — The Sons of Sam
**Logline.** Letters from Yonkers tie the cult to the murders in the newspapers, and the
connection is false in the way that matters and true in the way that does not.

**Opening.** Sayer's letters, copying Son of Sam imagery, circulate among employees and
local enthusiasts. The same symbol appears in three places: the Tagus archive, a CHOIR
diagram and a Vigil manuscript.

**Middle.** Fisk builds a nationwide theory. The Yonkers postmark becomes evidence.
Players can find that Sayer has no link to the murders and a genuine link to a rogue
Ascendant inside Royce and its interest in the stairway.

**Close.** The Board decides whether to hand Security's cult file to the federal liaison,
Mercer, who already wrongly believes the Vigil runs Royce.

**If nobody acts.** The file goes to Mercer and confirms his mistake; Fisk's theory
becomes the company's.

**How players bend it.** Trace the correspondence to its Royce end; discredit or exploit
Fisk; keep the file in the company or send it out.

**Carries forward.** Whether Sayer's real link to the Ascendant cell is exposed.

## Episode 07 — SENTINEL
**Logline.** The federal program behind Royce's money is revealed, its sponsor wants the
work faster, and the Board turns on its own leadership.

**Opening.** Mercer's file lands. PROGRAM SENTINEL, created in 1969 to verify, assess
and contain, is revealed to the players.

**Middle.** A sponsor demands acceleration. Hensley's numbers do not close: OMEGA has
outrun its contractual authority. Inside the fence, Baines wants independent federal
control and is preparing a contingency for the Basin site (scenario-level; no weapon
exists). Control of Tagus becomes political.

**Close.** The Board votes no confidence in Royce or in Vale: the constitutional crisis.
The Board starts 4–3 for continuing OMEGA, so one persuaded or dismissed director flips
it.

**If nobody acts.** Leadership holds and the sponsor's acceleration is accepted.

**How players bend it.** Expose the overrun; persuade or remove a director; side with
the sponsor, with Royce, or with the officials who want the program dismantled.

**Carries forward.** The leadership of Royce; the federal bid for control.

## Episode 08 — The Minot Winter
**Logline.** International researchers arrive as a rumour of foreign occupation reaches
the local militia, and three kinds of helicopter meet over Tagus.

**Opening.** IGCC researchers arrive. The UN-invasion rumour, born inside Corporate
Security in 1976, leaks to a local militia.

**Middle.** Royce's contractor, federal teams and something that is not a conventional
aircraft are all reported over Tagus at once. An Ascendant cell's speculation that a
nuclear-scale event could force convergence surfaces (scenario-level only; nobody has a
weapon). The IGCC's interest traces to a source inside its own network, and to one
inside Royce.

**Close.** The Research Committee admits or expels the IGCC observer.

**If nobody acts.** The observer is expelled and the militia pressure rises.

**How players bend it.** Admit the observer; quiet or stoke the militia; work out which
helicopter was which; contain the nuclear rumour or let it spread.

**Carries forward.** Militia pressure; the IGCC inside or outside the fence; the status
of the nuclear rumour.

## Episode 09 — The Omega Hypothesis
**Logline.** Marsh's report comes back, or Marsh does, and the company has to decide
whether to keep building toward April.

**Opening.** Marsh's September report resurfaces (data compromised, early records
falsified, suspend the chamber), or Marsh herself does. Letters from Kessler.

**Middle.** CHOIR's prediction tests. News of John Carr's death at Minot (February 17,
1978) reaches the fiction and unsettles every theory; nobody in the fiction caused it.
How Marsh came to hold the original Vetch date is answered in this episode.

**Close.** The Research Committee decides whether to resume full OMEGA preparations or
suspend them pending review.

**If nobody acts.** Preparations resume on schedule.

**How players bend it.** Bring Marsh or her report before the committee; identify
Kessler as the IGCC's source; vote to suspend.

**Carries forward.** Marsh's status; Kessler's identity as a source; the trajectory of
the Index.

## Episode 10 — Drawing 68-24
**Logline.** The blueprint for the chamber turns out to show the stairway, and to depict
a machine built from itself.

**Opening.** Drawing 68-24 is found to match the stairway's geometry. A Keeper inside the
company recognizes it as Clara Wren's 1921 diagram.

**Middle.** The old concrete in Sublevel Nine is dated: it predates the building. The
1984 chamber is reached or reported, and its documents (a 1978 Research Committee vote,
people who have not yet joined the company) enter play as predictions players can
fulfil, prevent or falsify. How the chamber is reached, other than at the seventh
threshold, is still open and is designed for this episode.

**Close.** The Board decides who holds the drawing and the chamber keys.

**If nobody acts.** The drawing and keys stay with the scientific leadership.

**How players bend it.** Take the drawing; read, hide or publish the 1984 records;
decide who holds the keys.

**Carries forward.** Whose hands the prophecy documents are in; whether the loop is
understood.

## Episode 11 — The Threshold
**Logline.** Every faction moves at once toward the vote the 1984 records already
describe.

**Opening.** The factions converge: Keepers pushing containment, Ascendants pushing to
open, SENTINEL attempting a seizure, the IGCC demanding an inspection.

**Middle.** The 1978 Research Committee vote recorded in the 1984 chamber is scheduled
for real. Players hold, or know of, records that claim to know its outcome, ending "The
prior record was incorrect. This is the third occurrence."

**Close.** The Research Committee votes on activating the apparatus.

**If nobody acts.** The vote goes the way the scientific leadership wants.

**How players bend it.** Fulfil the prophecy, prevent it, or make it false; decide who
will be inside the chamber.

**Carries forward.** The vote's outcome against the prophecy; who is inside the chamber.

## Episode 12 — The Opening
**Logline.** April 1978: the test.

**Opening.** The full-scale OMEGA test approaches as a scheduled threat, not a
guaranteed event.

**Middle.** Players proceed, sabotage, seize, dismantle, or do something nobody planned.
The stairway's fate is decided alongside the apparatus.

**Close.** No collective decision is scripted. The engine resolves the ending from world
state: the Convergence Index band, the apparatus (intact, modified, sabotaged, seized),
who may legally run it, who is in the chamber and what they believe, and containment at
Tagus. Each combination maps to a consequence class written before Episode 11, so the
ending is never invented after players act. Whether the Presence is benevolent, and
whether its emergence is inevitable, stay open until then.

**If nobody acts.** The test runs on the scientific leadership's terms, and the Index
decides what that means.

**How players bend it.** Every lever in the season ends here.

**Carries forward.** Season Two: Crestone, the Omega geography, and what the Presence
has become.
