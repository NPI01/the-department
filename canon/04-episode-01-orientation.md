## The episode as a new hire sees it
You arrive at Station R with a cohort of new employees. The company's problems look
ordinary: a budget review, a failed prototype test, a scientist who transferred out in a
hurry, a technician who died in July, a major investor's tour. Then small things stop
agreeing with each other. A requisition refers to a specimen nobody can find. A security
memo describes a helicopter landing where no flight was scheduled. The sheriff writes to
ask why company trucks were parked beside a dead steer. Your campus map is not the same
as the one on your colleague's desk.

In Round 3 the cohort elects two of its own to the employee seats on the Research and
Safety Committees. In Round 7 the Board votes on a motion to restructure departmental
reporting lines, and everyone with an opinion is lobbying the directors. By Round 14,
what you have found, whom you have told, and what the Board decided are permanent.

The laboratory inventory for the HARVEST tissue preservation unit is critically low. Immediate replacement of Lot 77-B is required to maintain sample viability. *The wrong detail: Ely writes as if Marsh were still expected on this handoff. Marsh's file says she transferred on September 29; the memo is dated October 3. Either Ely's records are stale or the transfer date is.*

## Irregularity 3: Sheriff's Letter
**SLUG:** shfr-call-9910
**FROM:** Sheriff Dale Hollis, Rural County Sheriff's Department
**TO:** Harold Sloane, Director of Corporate Security
**DATE:** October 6, 1977
**SUBJECT:** Inquiry: Royce Vehicles at Dead Rancher Site
**CLASSIFICATION:** PUBLIC RECORD

Mr. Sloane,
This is Dale Hollis. I'm writing this from the office after the morning rounds. We had a call from Gary Vane at Vane Cattle Co. He's claiming one of your trucks was parked near his north pasture this morning, right next to a steer that turned up dead yesterday. The animal was drained. No blood. Clean cuts.
Vane is agitated. He says your guys were using a special light, not a flashlight, something blue. He saw a Royce badge on the fender. I drove out to check. The steer is there. I didn't see any trucks. I didn't see any blue lights. But I know Royce has a way of making things disappear.
Just a heads up. If your people are out here playing jokes on the locals again, I'm going to start writing tickets. Tell them to stay off the private land.
Dale Hollis
Sheriff

*The wrong detail: the steer "turned up dead yesterday" but the sheriff "drove out to check" this morning and found it undisturbed by scavengers. A carcass on open prairie is never undisturbed overnight.*

## NPC Letter 1: Elmer Bernard

**FROM:** Elmer Bernard, Tagus
**TO:** [New Hire Name]
**DATE:** October 2, 1977
**SUBJECT:** N/A

Dear Friend,
I found your name on the list Royce dropped off at the post office this morning. They said you're new people. New blood. That's good, maybe. The old stories say the ground likes fresh feet.
I wanted to tell you about the hum. It's not the wind. The wind is sharp, like glass. This is low. It's in the teeth. It comes up from the earth when the sun goes down. I hear it in the church bell tower. The iron ring vibrates. My cat won't come inside after dark.
Don't go out at night. They watch you. Not the men in uniforms. The ground itself. It knows when you're listening.
Elmer Bernard
St. Olaf Church, Tagus

## NPC Letter 3: Capt. Thomas Bradley
**SLUG:** memo-bradley-03
**FROM:** Capt. Thomas Bradley, Chief Pilot, Prairie Meridian Aviation
**TO:** Col. Frank Baines, Director, Aerospace Systems
**DATE:** October 12, 1977
**SUBJECT:** Instrument Interference on Flight 77-ON

Col. Baines,
Flight 77-ON experienced significant instrument failure at 1900 hours on October 10. We were conducting the scheduled calibration run over Sector 4. At 1,500 feet, both magnetic compasses spun. The altimeter dropped by 2,000 feet for ten seconds before resetting. The VHF radio picked up a signal that was not in our frequency bank. It was not static. It was a voice.
It repeated a single coordinate pair. We forced a landing at Minot AFB for safety check. The instruments are now reading normal. The co-pilot is shaken. He hears humming in his sleep.
I am requesting a review of our flight path. Sector 4 has been marked 'Restricted' by Royce Security for reasons unknown. I believe we flew too close to the edge.
Respectfully,
Capt. Thomas Bradley

*The wrong detail: Bradley logs a forced landing at Minot AFB "for safety check" at 1900 on October 10. The base log for that evening shows no civilian landing. The coordinate pair, if a player asks Bradley for it, is a point four miles south of Tagus.*
