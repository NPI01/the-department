# Field Guide for New Employees

> **DRAFT v0.1 (2026-10-11), PROPOSED until Nathan confirms.** Gameplay tips for
> players, written by Claude from `docs/rulebook-draft.md`; it adds no rules and no
> story. Published at `/guide` and linked from every letter the game sends. Where it
> mentions a [PROPOSED] rule, it follows the rulebook draft.

## Your first week

**Read your file before you write to anyone.** Your department decides which files you
can open, and your specialization decides what you are good at. Your background tells
you what you would rather nobody knew. Everything you do should start from those.

**Introduce yourself.** Nobody is told which names in the directory belong to players.
A short, polite note to everyone in your department costs nothing and tells you who
answers like a person.

**Spend the first rounds on questions, not on answers.** `INVESTIGATE` and
`REQUEST_FILES` are cheap. Learn what your department can see that others cannot,
because that is what you will trade.

**Vote in the Round 3 election.** The cohort elects two of its own to the committees.
A seat is the first real power a new hire can hold, and holding one changes who writes
to you.

## Every day

**Write early, commit late.** Letters are free and arrive within minutes; orders only
count at the 8:00 PM lock. Use the day to find out what your orders should be.

**Hold on purpose, never by accident.** `HOLD` keeps your standing orders running and
pays 5 extra AU tomorrow. Sending nothing pays nothing and suspends them. If you are
busy, send one line: `HOLD`.

**Bank for the moment that matters.** AU cap at 60. Three quiet rounds of holding leave
you room for the night you need to `OBTAIN`, `SURVEIL` and `RECRUIT` at once.

**Read your dispatch to the end.** The ruling on every order says what happened and
why. When something failed, the reason is usually the next thing worth investigating.

## Writing to people

**Use the addresses in your dispatch.** They carry your seal. A letter without it is
delivered but cannot carry orders or votes, and anyone holding your sealed addresses
could write as you, so never forward them.

**Put commitments on their own line.** `ORDER:`, `VOTE`, `HOLD`, `ACCEPT AGREEMENT`.
Anything else is conversation, and the game will never act on a guess.

**Choose the kind of letter.** A `MEMO:` is on the record and can be cited in
committee. A private letter can be denied. A `SEALED:` dispatch is harder to intercept
and draws attention for exactly that reason.

**Assume every letter can be forwarded.** Whatever you write to someone becomes theirs
to show. Say the dangerous things at a `MEET`: nothing said in person is on paper,
which means nobody can quote it as evidence, including you.

**Staff have memories.** The people the game plays remember promises, slights and who
reported whom. They answer on their own schedules, and some never answer at all. A
silence is information too.

## Evidence and committees

**Know the three grades.** A rumour is intelligence. A document with provenance is
evidence. Only a committee makes a finding. Do not stake a vote on a rumour.

**Provenance is everything.** Where a document came from, who held it and how you got
it matter as much as what it says. `EXAMINE` what you hold before you rely on it.

**A finding can be official and wrong.** Committees rule on what is filed, not on what
is true. Records can be authentic and still contradict each other.

**Count votes before you sponsor.** A motion stays open two to five rounds. Use them to
lobby. Ties keep things as they were, and abstaining counts as voting.

## Deals

**Formal agreements are enforced; promises are remembered.** A numbered agreement is
carried out by the game where it can be. A promise in a letter is only as good as the
person who made it, and the game remembers who kept theirs.

**Get it in writing when it matters, and in person when it must not exist.**

## When you are away

**Going away? Say so.** `LEAVE 3` steps you out for three rounds without damaging your
standing. Three missed turns in a row put you on leave anyway, with less grace.

## Fair play

**Keep real life out of it.** Characters lie, threaten and betray. Players do not. Never
use another player's real details in play.

**When a ruling looks wrong, appeal it.** Every result cites its rule. If you think the
rule was misapplied, say so before the next lock.
